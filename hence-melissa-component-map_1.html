Priority components

A priority is a discrete outcome that helps a Hence user make progress against improving their financial health and/or achieving a personal goal. Priorities may contain:

Target

Attributes that define whether or not the priority has been achieved. This can contain specific financial thresholds, securing insurance coverage, or definition of a complex end goal like retirement or home buying where a target may include the downpayment, the mortgage rate, the location, and attributes about the house. Targets may be suggested by Hence but are refined and agreed upon by the user. Targets may be set by a step that opens a conversation with Hence.

Targets appear (and can be edited) on Priority detail pages and are generated in conversation with Hence.

Step

A discrete, single-step task that can be completed by the user or completed by Hence. Steps are organized into prioritized lists called Action Packs or may appear as individual tasks on a priority detail page. Steps are the smallest component in the content system, and the home page curates the top three steps the user should take at any given time. Completing a step may require asking hence to do work on your behalf, engaging in a conversation with Hence to make a decision, or gathering specific documentation or data. CTAs for steps include "Send to Hence" for work Hence can complete autonomously, "Talk it through" for decisions, or "Add information" when the user needs to send in information.

Action Pack

A task that requires more than one Step to complete. Action packs appear in a prioritized list on the Priority Detail Page and in the Plan section of the product in the Actions tab. Tapping an action pack opens the Action Pack Detail Page, which includes the action title and a prioritized list of Steps associated with that action. Actions are always associated with at least one priority.

Task List

A prioritized list of action packs and/or steps a user completes to achieve their target. Task lists appear on the Priority Detail Page.

Rules

  • Two named, then overflow. Full names wrap and stop reading as an eyebrow at 390px.
  • No label. The slot does the work; people learn it.
  • The engine shortens for display. Priorities are user-generated and renameable, so abbreviation is a rendering concern, never a second name to maintain.
  • Always plural in form. Taking the grammatical hit on the single-priority case keeps the pattern teaching the model on every card.
  • Tap resolves the overflow. Opens the Action Pack Detail Page and its associated-priorities module.
  • Carried wherever nothing else names the priority. On Ask Home, in the plan. Inside an Action Pack or a task list the container has already named it.

Composition rules

R1
A task is the same item wherever it appears.Completing it in one place completes it everywhere, because there is only one of it.
R2
An Action Pack appears on every priority it belongs to.Always at least one, often more. Not duplication — the same component rendered wherever it's relevant.
R3
Every card names the priority it's working on.The alternative is organising by a system nobody understands. Being imprecise about how many priorities a task belongs to is recoverable; being unreadable is not.
R4
The Priority Detail page is an assembly, not a template.Target and task list are the spine; every other module is conditional on the priority.
R5
The step is what surfaces on Ask Home.Not the pack, not the priority. Home shows the smallest unit a person can finish.
R6
A step standing alone in a list is the exception.Packs are the norm in a task list and in the plan.
