Definitions

Priority

A discrete outcome that helps a Hence user make progress against improving their financial health and/or achieving a personal goal. Priorities are mostly generated by completing the Foundations process or as the result of an Ask conversation, and can also be created by the user directly. They are renameable. A priority is reached when its Target is met; some priorities, like improving your cash margin, are ongoing and never complete.

A priority contains a Target and a To-Do List, plus whatever other modules its subject requires.

Target

Attributes that define whether or not the priority has been achieved. This can contain specific financial thresholds, securing insurance coverage, or definition of a complex end goal like retirement or home buying where a target may include the downpayment, the mortgage rate, the location, and attributes about the house. Targets may be discrete and finite, or ongoing, like an optimization. Targets may be suggested by Hence but are refined and agreed upon by the user. Targets may be set by a step that opens a conversation with Hence.

Targets appear (and can be edited) on Priority detail pages and are generated in conversation with Hence.

Action

A unit of work that requires more than one Task to complete. Actions capture work that can occur over long or short horizons, including with breaks for necessary progress or events. Actions appear in a prioritized list on the Priority Detail Page and in the To-Do List in My Hence. Tapping an action opens the Action Detail Page, which includes the action title and a prioritized list of the Tasks it contains. Actions are always associated with at least one priority, and may be associated with several. An action card carries its task count and its duration together. Because an action has an order and an end state, it can be handed to Hence to run: Hence completes what it can on its own and brings the user in only where a decision or an input is needed.

Task

A discrete unit of work that can be completed by the user or by Hence in a discrete session. Tasks are organized into prioritized lists called Actions or may appear as individual tasks on a priority detail page. Tasks are the smallest component with a card of their own, and Ask Home curates the top three tasks the user should take at any given time. Completing a task may require asking Hence to do work on your behalf, engaging in a conversation with Hence to make a decision, or gathering specific documentation or data. CTAs for tasks include "Send to Hence" for work Hence can complete autonomously, "Talk it through" for decisions, or "Add information" when the user needs to send in information.

Open Whether every CTA opens Ask. Some instruction sets read better as static content — a call script being the clear case — and Frank’s library is written that way. See R17.

Instruction

The smallest, most discrete steps required to complete a task. Instructions always appear as a series to be completed in one sitting, and only surface once a task is underway. They have no card design and no independent existence: they carry no eyebrow and can’t be re-prioritized. The task subhead describes its instructions in a single sentence.

Instructions carry their own detail. Guidance on how to complete a step — which option to choose, what the confirmation screen will say, what to say when the menu answers — is instruction, however long it runs. It does not become a different kind of content by being specific.

Delivered in one of four modes: delegating work to Hence, asking the user to supply documents or information, working through a decision or trade-off, or walking the user through a multi-step process. Open Whether all four run in Ask, or some are served as static content.

Tip

Content attached to an instruction that is not itself a step. A tip is never completed — it governs how a step is done rather than being part of doing it: a warning, a script, a calculator, a definition.

The relationship runs one way: every tip belongs to an instruction, but an instruction does not require one. A tip is attached where there is something to attach, not authored as a field on every step.

Because nothing in a tip is finished, it is not bound to one sequence. The same tip attaches wherever that kind of step occurs, which makes tips a small shared library rather than something authored per task.

Open Dawn’s definition scopes tips to “an instruction or task”. A task-level tip is plausible — a calculator that serves the whole task rather than one step — but it has no place to render yet.

A tip is not a level. It renders inside the instruction it belongs to, visually distinct so it does not read as another step. See the full argument ›

Monitoring

Work Hence does continuously, without being asked and without producing anything to complete. Monitoring watches a condition and does nothing until that condition changes: a rate that could be beaten, a renewal date approaching, a balance drifting from its target allocation.

Monitoring is a module on a Priority Detail page, not a unit of work. It fails the completion test — there is nothing in it to finish — so it cannot be a task, an action or a priority, and it cannot be handed to Hence to run because Hence is already running it.

When the condition it watches changes, monitoring produces work: a new task or action inside the priority it belongs to. That is the only way it enters the plan. Between those moments it shows what is being watched and when it was last checked, so background work stays visible.

Open Whether what it emits is always a task, or whether some conditions warrant a proposed priority of their own.

To-Do List

A prioritized list of actions and/or tasks. Any group of actions and tasks is a to-do list, so the module appears wherever such a group is shown: on the Priority Detail Page, where it holds the work for that priority, and in My Hence, where it holds everything across the plan.

The To-Do List in My Hence holds every action and task across the plan, so it carries search and filtering — by priority, and by time: this week, due soon, urgent.

Completing tasks with Ask

Every task CTA lands in Ask. A task begins on a card and ends when it is settled — delegated work comes back to be approved, decisions are made by choosing, and nothing Hence finishes interrupts what the user is doing. The session log in the top right holds what has been done and decided, and is where work in progress waits.

1
Send to Hence
Two tasks on the Action Detail page. The user taps Send to Hence on the first — work Hence can do alone.
2
Talk it through
The sent card shrinks into the session log and the next task moves up. Tapping it opens Ask, the surface turns plum, and the log carries across showing work running.
3
Hence finishes
The log fills and turns lilac. Nothing else changes — the user is mid-decision and is not pulled out of it.
4
Settle the decision
The decision is recorded as a completed task, not as another message, and Ask offers the next question rather than dead-ending.
5
Open the log
Tapping the log opens a roll-up of what has been finished and decided, with the quotes waiting at the top.
6
Review inline
What Hence prepared opens in the same session as a comparison, with the way back visible.
7
Approve
Approving completes the task — recorded the same way as the first — and the session picks up where it left off.

Can you complete it?
YesIt is an instruction. “Pick roll over an employer plan” is something you do and then have done. It takes a place in the sequence.
NoIt is a tip. “Dial the number on your own statement” is never finished — it is a rule that governs a step rather than being one.

What the Tip column is really doing

In every row, the Instruction says what to do and the Tip says how. Complete the paperwork, then which option to choose. Call and request a rollover, then what to say when the menu answers. Invest the money, then log in, find Trade, search, submit.

If the Tip is the detailed version of the Instruction, then the Instruction is a heading. That is what has happened on row three, where the four numbered steps sit in Tip and the word “Invest the moved money” sits in Instruction. Row two writes the call out twice for the same reason.

Which means a tip cannot simply be a more detailed instruction. Define it that way and every instruction needs one, and each pair is only ever a heading and the real content sitting under it.

What tips are actually for

A small typed set, written once and reused

The category is worth keeping. It is smaller than the column suggests, and holds different things from most of what ended up in it.

Warning
◆ Always dial the number printed on your own card or statement.
A risk that comes with a kind of step. Appears on every instruction that involves calling an institution.
Script
“I’ve had an offer from another card at 0%. What can you do on my rate?” — and what they are likely to say back.
Reusable because it is about a kind of conversation. “Ask for the rollovers department” is not a script in this sense — it only works on this one call.
Tool
A break-even calculator for a balance transfer fee against the rate saved.
Works anywhere the same maths comes up, so it is built once and attached where it is needed.
Reference
Direct vs indirect rollover: with indirect, 20% is withheld and you have 60 days to replace it.
A definition or a threshold. Worth linking to when it matters, not repeating inside every step that touches it.
Where this leaves Frank’s library

He keeps the content and gains reuse. The warnings, scripts and calculators are the tips. Typing them means each one is written once and pulled into every entry that needs it, rather than rewritten inside each.

The rest becomes instruction. Guidance does not stop being instruction because it runs long. It stays in the sequence, where someone working through the task will see it.

What we’d propose

Keep the distinction in the model, drop it from the hierarchy. The generator needs to know a tip is a tip — that governs whether it can be reordered, skipped, or dropped once a task is underway. The user needs one list.

Support content is a property of an instruction, not a level. Guidance on how to complete a step is instruction, however long it runs, and renders as part of that step.

A tip is reusable; an instruction is not. If it holds for a kind of step rather than one step, it is a tip: written once, held in a shared library, attached wherever that step appears. Cheaper to write and consistent everywhere it shows up.

Rules & tests

Telling them apart

Three tests, applied in order — each one only reached if the last said yes

The completion test

Can you complete it?
No
It is a tip.
A warning, a script, a calculator, a definition. There is nothing in it to finish, so it governs a step rather than being one. It has no place in the order and attaches to the instruction it belongs to.
Yes
It is work.
Which level of work depends on where it stops.

The boundary test

Can you stop here and have something?
No
It is inside a task. It is an instruction.
Instructions are the series of steps required to complete a task. Stop halfway and nothing is done. This is why an instruction has no card, no eyebrow, and cannot be re-prioritized: a half-finished series has produced nothing to show.
Yes
It is a task, an action, or a priority.
Which one depends on scale.

The scale test

What is settled when this is done?
One thing
Task
One decision reached, or one change made. It stays settled after the user walks away. Completed in one sitting.
A phase of the work
Action
Several tasks that together move the work to a later stage: the account exists, the money has moved, the debt is consolidated. A phase must be nameable without listing what is in it. If it cannot be named that way, it is not a phase.
An outcome
Priority
The financial or personal result the work is for. Finite, or ongoing like an optimization.

Instructions execute decisions. They do not make them. Where a step appears to contain a choice, the choice was made at the task level: "request a Direct Rollover" is an instruction because direct versus indirect was settled above it.

Completing one level does not complete the level above it. Finishing a task does not finish its action; finishing an action does not finish its priority. Instructions are the exception, and that is the whole point of the boundary: the last instruction in a series does complete its task, because the series was only ever one thing.

Rules

  • Two named, then overflow. Full names wrap and stop reading as an eyebrow at 390px.
  • No label. Nothing reads “Priorities:”. The position is consistent enough to learn in a screen or two, and a label would cost more room than the second name.
  • The engine shortens for display. Priorities are user-generated and renameable, so abbreviation is a rendering concern, never a second name to maintain.
  • Always the same slot, even for one. A card serving a single priority still names it there. The slot reads the same way every time, so nobody has to work out whether this card is the exception.
  • Carried by both cards. On Ask Home and in a to-do list a card can appear far from its priority, so it names it. Dropped on the Action Detail Page, where the context is already set.
  • Tap resolves the overflow. Opens the Action Detail Page and its associated-priorities module.

Composition rules

R1
A task is the same item wherever it appears.Completing it in one place completes it everywhere, because there is only one of it.
R2
An action appears on every priority it belongs to.Always at least one, often more. Not duplication — the same component rendered wherever it's relevant.
R3
Every card names the priorities it belongs to.A card can appear a long way from its priority — on Ask Home, in a filtered to-do list, on a Foundations area. Without the eyebrow the user has no way of knowing what the work is for.
R4
The Priority Detail page is an assembly, not a template.Target and to-do list are the spine; every other module is conditional on the priority.
R5
The task is what surfaces on Ask Home.Not the action, not the priority. Home shows the smallest unit a person can finish.
R6
A card is read by its head and its foot.A priority is the only card with a rank in the eyebrow and a progress bar in the foot. An action names its priorities in the eyebrow and ends in a task count and a chevron. A task also names its priorities but ends in a CTA. Priorities and actions open a page; a task opens Ask.
R7
Hence can be given a task or a whole action, never a to-do list.A task goes to Hence through its own CTA. An action goes as a sequence, through the H button on its detail page, because it has an order and an end state. A to-do list is ranked rather than sequenced and completing it settles nothing, so it filters and searches instead.
R8
A task standing alone in a list is the exception.Actions are the norm in a to-do list.
R9
Plum is for deciding, cream is for the record.Plum surfaces are the working environment — Ask, and Home, which is an Ask surface. Cream surfaces hold what has been decided and what still needs work. Inside Ask, cream blocks are the record being written: a scored area, a settled decision, a proposed priority.
R10
A Financial Area is a measurement, not an outcome.An area cannot own priorities, because a priority is a result and an area is a reading. Areas offer actions; the priorities are what the user saves. This is why the Foundations pages list Actions for you and not a plan.
R11
Grey, never partial.An area with insufficient data reads grey and says what it needs. It never shows a number it has not earned — a half-calculated score would change every target derived from it, and the user has no way of knowing which ones.
R12
Hence prepares; the user authorises.Nothing that changes real-world state completes on Hence’s side. It finishes the work, puts it in the session log, and the task closes when the user approves it — not when Hence stopped working.
R13
The account comes after the value, never before it.The ask to sign up is the ask to keep something. It lands once there is a plan worth keeping, which is also the first moment it can be explained in one line.
R14
Guidance on how to complete a step is instruction, however long it runs.Detail does not make content a different type. Which option to choose, what the confirmation screen says, what to say when the menu answers — all of it is the instruction, and it stays in the sequence.
R15
A tip is not a level.Nothing lives inside a tip, so it adds no depth to the hierarchy. It renders inside the instruction it belongs to, marked so it does not read as another step.
R15a
Every tip belongs to an instruction; not every instruction has one.The relationship is one-way and optional. A tip is attached where there is something to attach, not authored as a field on every step.
R16
Instructions render as one connected series.Numbered rows in a single block, not separate cards. A card implies the thing can be picked up, moved or re-prioritized, and an instruction can be none of those.
R17
Not every task CTA opens Ask.Some instruction sets are easier to follow as static content than as a conversation — a call script being the clear case. The mode is chosen per instruction, not fixed by the object. Open at V1.
